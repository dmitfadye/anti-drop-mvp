# Discovery, audience and metrics

Problem hypothesis: someone receives unexpected money or an urgent forwarding request and cannot understand the pattern or independently verify it. Priority recruitable segment: adults new to Russian retail banking, including non-native Russian speakers. Membership does not imply fraud risk. Secondary: inexperienced adults encountering unfamiliar requests. Buyer hypothesis: retail safety/customer-experience owner, not AML replacement.

JTBD: When an unfamiliar transfer/request makes me uncertain, help me understand observable signals and verify independently, so I can choose a safe next step without panic or unjustified accusations.

Value hypothesis: contextual explanation and a clear safe-action path improve correct action over generic guidance at identical signals. Candidate differentiation: deterministic explanations, simple language, reviewed optional translation and durable test-case trail. VTB already has safety guidance, education and trusted-person mechanisms (E05). Incremental benefit must be measured; detector superiority is not claimed.

## Falsifiable hypotheses (all unvalidated)

| Hypothesis | Validation | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| H1: verification is recurring and unmet | 12..20 adult formative interviews/tasks, concrete experiences | Few concrete incidents or existing flow sufficient |
| H2: context improves safe action | Matched generic/contextual tasks, counterbalanced order | No improvement or more unsafe actions |
| H3: simple/reviewed translated text improves comprehension | RU+one native-reviewed language, teach-back | Persistent/worse misunderstanding |
| H4: case path reduces support friction | Timed synthetic retrieval/navigation comparison | More time/abandonment without clearer next step |
| H5: bank can supply timely signals and fund scope | Authorized risk/CX feasibility interview | Only post-loss signals or excessive cost |

## Twelve neutral interview questions

1. Tell me about the last unfamiliar money request you received.
2. What happened before you chose what to do?
3. What information did you have then?
4. How did you decide whether to trust it?
5. What did you verify, and where?
6. What made that easy or difficult?
7. Did you seek help; how did you find the contact?
8. Which words/screens were unclear?
9. Which language would you choose, and why?
10. Show your action in this fictional scenario and explain your reasoning.
11. What does this warning explain, and what remains uncertain?
12. What would make you ignore it or stop using it?

Recruit consenting adults through permitted community/university channels. No real account screenshots, names, transactions or fraud disclosure required. Use coded observations with retention/withdrawal rules. Do not ask leading usefulness questions. Separate observed behavior from stated preference and report negative cases.

## Market/reach: assumptions, not counts

TAM: Russian digital-bank users encountering this task, count/frequency unknown. SAM: supported-language users reachable via bank-owned consented channel, unknown. SOM: actual approved rollout exposures, unknown. Do not equate all bank clients or sector fraud losses with obtainable market.

Illustrative exposure=eligible*digital eligibility*language share*rollout. Conservative:100000*.5*.1*.1=500; base:250000*.6*.2*.2=6000; optimistic:500000*.7*.3*.3=31500. Every input is unverified, not a VTB statistic. Exposure is not willing adoption, revenue or prevented fraud. Planned12..20 participants are separate from market reach.

## Metrics and experiment

Primary safe_action_rate=predefined correct independent-verification choices/valid task exposures. Clicking a case button is not fraud prevention. Secondary: teach-back comprehension, median safe-action time, completion, dismissal and support-path abandonment. Guardrails: unsafe action, unwarranted fear/accusation, false alerts on legitimate stories, accessibility failure and data disclosure.

Baseline/effect unknown. Prospective hypothesis:+10 percentage points safe action without >5-point decline in legitimate-task completion. Use matched tasks, randomized/counterbalanced order, identical signals, generic vs contextual text and blinded coding where practical. Report denominators, intervals, exclusions/missing data. Formative12..20 sessions diagnose usability, not fraud reduction. Later powered trial needs measured baseline, justified effect and repeated-measures/clustering adjustment; never size it from handmade detector recall.
